---
title: "Mono no aware（物の哀れ）"
date: "2026-11-10"
description: "Why I stay away from things that can be ruined forever, and why I like work that can still change."
---

I don't own a well-made car, or a watch you can feel on your wrist, or other things someone has looked after for years. It isn't only the money. What I can't bear is how they can deteriorate. That feeling makes me shudder. They can get scratched, dented, and I can't take it. The object is still there, but it is no longer the same, and that possibility is enough to keep it away from me.

In Japanese there is a word for that emotion: mono no aware (物の哀れ), a sensitivity to the fact that beautiful things wear down and come to an end. It usually comes with a calm, almost bittersweet acceptance. The beauty is also supposed to lie in the fact that it does not last. In me, the shudder is the same, but it does not end in calm. It ends in not owning them. I would rather not have the thing than live with the first scratch.

The strange part is that this only happens to me with things that are meant to stay intact. With software, it does not happen at all.

I love to build. And, if necessary, to tear it down and start again. To watch it evolve. A program does not have a single body to protect. If something does not work, you change it, you throw it out, you make it again, and sometimes it comes out better than before. The same is true at the bench: I like opening things, trying, failing, and starting over. A board I can mess with is not a watch. Deterioration does not sit in my chest the same way, because there is no unique, irreplaceable piece where the mark has to remain. In that kind of work, building and destroying are the same thing.

That is the part I enjoy most: making something, seeing how it changes, and starting over when that is what it needs. I like work that can still change. That is why I stay away from things that can be ruined forever, and, on the same day, delete a piece of a system or take something apart and start over.
